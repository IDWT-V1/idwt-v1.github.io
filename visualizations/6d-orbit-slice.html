Orbital Lobes from a Projective 6D Orbit

A conserved Hamiltonian flow can produce the aligned \(s/p/d/f\) pattern exactly—provided every displayed quantity survives the projective quotient. Here is the construction, its proof, the live geometry, and the line between established mathematics and unfinished physics.

What has to be respected

A six-real-dimensional particle does not imply that all of reality has six dimensions. In IDWT, \(d=6\) identifies the electron sector: the particle has structure across six directions, while an interaction reads the coordinates shared with the other participant. The local angular geometry can be represented by \(\mathbb{CP}^3\), because four complex homogeneous coordinates contain eight real components and the complex rescaling \(z\sim cz\) removes two real degrees of freedom:

\[ \dim_{\mathbb R}\mathbb{CP}^3=2(4)-2=6. \]

This is a local projective model of the sector's orientation data, not a claim that the electron's entire global space is the compact manifold \(\mathbb{CP}^3\). More importantly, a formula on homogeneous coordinates is physical only if it is unchanged by \(z\mapsto cz\). A common phase is gauge; it cannot supply the angle seen by an observer.

The conserved projective flow

For a Hermitian \(4\times4\) matrix \(M\), the expectation value

\[ H([z])=\frac{z^\dagger Mz}{z^\dagger z} \]

is well defined on projective space and generates the Hamiltonian flow

\[ [z(t)]=[e^{-iMt}z(0)]. \]

The evolution is norm preserving, and \(H\) is constant because \(M\) commutes with its own exponential. For the angular family, choose the traceless generator and initial point

\[ M_L=\omega \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac14&L&0&0\\ L&\tfrac14&0&0\\ 0&0&-\tfrac34&0\\ 0&0&0&\tfrac14 \end{pmatrix},\qquad z(0)=\left(A,0,\frac{B}{\sqrt2},\frac{B}{\sqrt2}\right), \qquad A,B>0,\quad A^2+B^2=1. \]

With \(\theta=\omega t\), exponentiation is elementary:

\[ z_1=Ae^{-i\theta/4}\cos(L\theta),\quad z_2=-iAe^{-i\theta/4}\sin(L\theta),\quad z_3=\frac{B}{\sqrt2}e^{3i\theta/4},\quad z_4=\frac{B}{\sqrt2}e^{-i\theta/4}. \]

The \(z_1,z_2\) block supplies an \(L\)-fold amplitude exchange. The \(z_3,z_4\) pair supplies a clock through a relative phase. Neither relies on the discarded common phase. At \(\theta=2\pi\), all four components differ from their initial values by the same factor \(-i\), so the full projective orbit—not merely its picture—is closed after one turn.

The aligned-projection theorem

Define a nonnegative radius and an angular clock by

\[ r([z])=\frac{|z_1|}{\sqrt{|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2}},\qquad e^{i\phi([z])}=\frac{z_3\bar z_4}{|z_3z_4|}. \]

Under \(z\mapsto cz\), each numerator and denominator acquires the same magnitude factor; both quantities are unchanged. Along the flow, \(r=|\cos(L\theta)|\) and \(e^{i\phi}=e^{i\theta}\). The observer-aligned plane map is therefore

\[ X+iY=Rr([z])e^{i\phi([z])} =R|\cos(L\theta)|e^{i\theta}. \]

Exact count

For \(L>0\), the radius is maximal at \(\theta=k\pi/L\) and zero at \(\theta=(k+\tfrac12)\pi/L\). One full turn therefore contains exactly \(2L\) separated radial maxima.

The four families

\(L=0\) gives a ring. Then \(L=1,2,3\) give two, four and six lobes: the familiar macro-shapes labelled \(s,p,d,f\). This count is an exact property of the stated map.

How higher-dimensional magnetism generates the lock

A magnetic field is fundamentally a curvature two-form, not a three-dimensional arrow. On the full manifold its components are \(F_{AB}\), and minimal coupling contains

\[ H_{\rm mag}=-\frac{q}{2m_e}\sum_{A<B}F_{AB}L^{AB}. \]

Scalar electric charge therefore does not force the field to act as a common phase. Spatially varying connection components can rotate the electron's orientation in any supported coordinate two-plane. The photon's own two-dimensionality fixes its transverse mode; it does not confine electromagnetic contact to only two dimensions of a higher-dimensional particle.

Hopf flux supplies the integer level

For normalized \(Z\in S^7\) over \(\mathbb{CP}^3\), the canonical connection and curvature are

\[ \mathcal A=-iZ^\dagger dZ,\qquad \mathcal F=d\mathcal A,\qquad \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\mathcal F=1. \]

The degree-\(L\) bundle consequently has \(c_1(\mathcal O(L))=L\). Its state space is not numerology: homogeneous polynomial combinatorics gives the exact representation

\[ H^0(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(L)) \cong\operatorname{Sym}^L(\mathbb C^4)^*,\qquad \dim=\binom{L+3}{3}. \]

The central Hopf \(U(1)\) supplies this integral flux level. The electron still has charge \(-e\): \(L\) labels the background bundle and its state representation, not a charge \(-Le\).

One isometry supplies the whole matrix

Now act within that representation using one shell-independent \(U(1)\) isometry of the four fundamental coordinates,

\[ G=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,0). \]

This is a Hamiltonian isometry of \(\mathbb{CP}^3\). Its moment map and classical flow are

\[ \mu_G([Z])=\frac{Z^\dagger GZ}{Z^\dagger Z},\qquad [Z]\longmapsto[e^{-i\theta G}Z]. \]

With \((2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega_{\rm FS}=1\), the prequantum curvature is \(-iL\omega_{\rm FS}\), the level-\(L\) moment map is \(L\mu_G\), and the holomorphic lift is

\[ \widehat G_L=\sum_{j=1}^4g_jz_j\frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}, \qquad \widehat G_Lz^a=(a\cdot g)z^a. \]

The curvature level and matrix action are therefore two parts of one symplectic construction. A central shift \(G\mapsto G+cI\) adds the same \(cL\) to every degree-\(L\) state and only changes the common projective phase.

For every \(L\geq1\), four degree-\(L\) monomials have the following induced weights:

Taking the even and odd combinations of the first pair rotates \(\operatorname{diag}(L,-L)\) into \(L\sigma_x\). Therefore, on this four-state subspace,

\[ G_L\big|_{\mathcal V_L} =L\sigma_x\oplus\operatorname{diag}(-1,0),\qquad M_L=\omega\left(G_L\big|_{\mathcal V_L}+\tfrac14I\right). \]

This reproduces the entire projective generator exactly for every \(L\geq1\). The scalar \(I/4\) is an energy-zero choice on each displayed four-state subspace, not one fixed fundamental central shift (which would lift as \(cLI\)). The amplitude exchange and clock are two blocks of one group action with one parameter \(\theta=\omega t\); their frequency lock is automatic. No separate frequency matching and no shell-by-shell fitted coefficient is involved.

The generator is unique at these premises

Let a shell-independent diagonal action have weights \((g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4)\). Requiring the two amplitude pure powers, the disjoint clock plane, and its primitive adjacent clock state gives

\[ g_1-g_4=1,\qquad g_2-g_4=-1,\qquad g_3-g_4=-1. \]

The constraint matrix has rank three; its only null direction is \((1,1,1,1)\), the invisible central phase. Therefore \(G=cI+\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,0)\) is unique up to that shift, orientation reversal and coordinate relabelling. In the clock plane, \(z_3^kz_4^{L-k}\) has relative weight \(-k\), so primitive unit winding uniquely gives \(k=1\).

The full unitary-equivalence family is the conjugacy orbit \(UGU^\dagger\). After subtracting its trace, the generator lies in \(\mathfrak{su}(4)\); the equivalence \(SU(4)\simeq\operatorname{Spin}(6)\) makes this the six-dimensional rotation orbit when the four coordinates carry that spinor action. An isotropic atom may then preserve the family while preparation or apparatus alignment selects a representative. In the locked extremal construction, a Chern sector \(k\) produces \(2|k|\) lobes, so matching the angular family labelled by \(2L\) forces \(|k|=L\).

Why an ordinary 3D field is not the source

The nucleus's leading Coulomb field is electrostatic, so \(\mathbf B=0\) in the nuclear rest frame. There is therefore no Zeeman generator at the gross-orbital scale. The unavoidable relativistic magnetic field seen in the electron's moving frame is approximately \(B_{\rm mot}=vE/c^2\). Using the Coulomb circular-orbit identities \(|e|E=m_ev^2/r\), \(\omega_{\rm orb}=v/r\), and \(v/c=\alpha/n\), its most favorable precession ratio is

\[ \frac{\omega_{\rm mot}}{\omega_{\rm orb}} =\frac{|e|B_{\rm mot}/(2m_e)}{v/r} =\frac12\frac{v^2}{c^2} =\frac{\alpha^2}{2n^2}. \]

With the IDWT value of \(\alpha\), this is only \(2.88\times10^{-5}\) for \(n=1\), and it falls as \(n^{-2}\). It is the fine-structure/spin-orbit scale, not the unit-frequency lock required by the visualization. Equivalently, forcing \(\omega_B=\omega_{\rm orb}\) would require

How much of the observer map is forced?

Write the amplitude occupation and primitive clock as

\[ q=\frac{|z_1|^2}{|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2},\qquad e^{i\phi}=\frac{z_3\bar z_4}{|z_3z_4|}. \]

Projective invariance and rotational covariance first give \(X+iY=Rh(q)e^{\pm i\phi}\), where a complex phase in \(h(q)\) would shear different radii through different angles. Reflection symmetry across the aligned apparatus axis removes that shear, giving

\[ X+iY=Rf(q)e^{\pm i\phi}. \]

If \(f(0)=0\) and \(f\) is strictly increasing, it preserves the maxima and zeros of \(q=\cos^2(L\theta)\), so every such detector response has exactly \(2L\) lobes. The displayed choice \(f(q)=\sqrt q\) follows from the additional intensity rule \((X^2+Y^2)/R^2=q\). Thus the lobe count is more robust than the particular radial shape.

Uniform Hamiltonian time gives the invariant occupation measure \(d\theta/(2\pi)\). Its amplitude fraction has density \([\pi\sqrt{q(1-q)}]^{-1}\), and the displayed radius has density \(2/[\pi\sqrt{R^2-r^2}]\). A finite observed cloud is the convolution of this curve measure with the apparatus response; its width still requires detector physics.

The s-ring obstruction

At \(L=0\), \(H^0(\mathbb{CP}^3,\mathcal O(0))=\mathbb C\), whose projectivization is a point. Zero Hopf flux cannot produce a relative-phase clock. The displayed orbit already has one unused coordinate and reduces exactly to

\[ M_0^{\rm min}=\omega\operatorname{diag}(0,-1,0),\qquad z_0^{\rm min}(0)=\left(A,\frac{B}{\sqrt2},\frac{B}{\sqrt2}\right), \]

one constant amplitude state plus the minimum two-state clock. This gives the ring exactly, but proves that its clock is universal extra structure rather than a consequence of Chern-zero magnetism. Its hidden-motion or ordinary-angular-momentum cancellation remains physical work.

See the projection

The default view looks straight down the chosen depth axis and therefore displays the theorem exactly. Dragging rotates the same curve, which is why the silhouette changes so strongly away from the aligned angle. The depth term is a transparent display choice,

\[ Z=\eta R\frac{|z_2|}{\sqrt{|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2}}\cos(3\phi) =\eta R|\sin(L\theta)|\cos(3\theta), \]

built from the same projectively invariant amplitude and clock. It gives the curve visible three-dimensional depth without changing the exact \(X,Y\) lobe count. It is not presented as a derived electron–detector coupling.

Where combinatorics enters

The lobe proof above uses the integer \(L\), but it does not invent that integer. Degree-\(L\) monomials in the four homogeneous coordinates have exponents \(a,b,c,d\geq0\) with \(a+b+c+d=L\). Stars-and-bars gives

\[ N_L=\binom{L+3}{3}=1,4,10,20,\ldots\quad(L=0,1,2,3,\ldots). \]

That is genuine combinatorial and representation-theoretic structure. It counts the full degree-\(L\) projective multiplet; it should not be confused with the \(1,2,4,6\) visible lobes of one aligned curve. The two sequences answer different questions.

What is established—and what is not

Status: exact mathematics inside a conditional physical identification. For every \(L\geq1\), the degree-\(L\) representation and its unique generator class derive \(M_L\) exactly. Representation matching forces \(|k|=L\); Hamiltonian conservation, projective invariance, the internal frequency lock, and the detector-response-robust \(2L\) lobe count then follow. The visualization implements those equations directly and contains no random phases or fitted lobe shapes.

The larger physical claim is not finished. A first-principles account must still derive why the bound system occupies a particular angular/flux sector, identify the curvature as the pullback of the physical full-manifold electromagnetic connection, determine \(\omega\), derive the complete three-dimensional detector embedding rather than choose a depth coordinate, and derive the finite transverse profile that turns a mathematical curve into an observed intensity distribution. A defensible smeared readout can be written

\[ \rho_{\rm obs}(x)=\frac1T\int_0^T G_\sigma\!\left(x-\Gamma_L(t)\right)dt, \]

where \(G_\sigma\) is a normalized finite-width field profile, but the physical value of \(\sigma\) and the detector coupling must come from the theory. Until those steps are supplied, the result is an exact geometric and representation-theoretic construction—not yet a complete derivation of atomic spectroscopy or of every textbook orbital surface.
