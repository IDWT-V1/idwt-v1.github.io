# Infinite Dimensional Wave Theory, Second Edition — Part 3: Forces and Couplings

## 1. One interaction, read four ways

Part 1 established the mechanism: there is one wave, its sectors share coordinates, and the wave's self-coupling — density against density on the shared coordinates — is the only interaction that exists. This Part works out what that single mechanism looks like from inside, and confronts its numbers with measurement.

What we call the different "forces" are the same self-coupling filtered through different sector geometry:

- **Electromagnetism** is the coupling read through the phase of the $d=2$ plane every charged particle contains.
- **The weak interaction** is the coupling read through the handedness of the complex sectors.
- **The strong interaction** is the coupling read through the three-fold class structure of the $d=4$ geometry, reaching only the quark sectors.
- **Gravity** is not a reading of the coupling at all — it is the curvature of the manifold itself (Part 4).

Who feels what follows from the nesting with no choices left. Every charged sector contains the $d=2$ plane, so electromagnetism is universal among the charged. Colour lives in the quark block $\{3,4\}$ and nowhere else: the neutrino's geometry projects colour to its neutral component, and the lepton geometries cancel it identically. The weak coupling reaches the left-handed part of every fermion. Gravity, sourced by mass, reaches everything.

## 2. Electromagnetism: the phase of the wave

The wave is complex, and a complex wave has a phase. The gradient of that phase, on the $d=2$ plane, *is* the electromagnetic potential; its curl is the electromagnetic field; the photon is the $d=2$ sector's ground mode, the ripple of pure phase with nothing excited — which is why its mass is exactly zero, and why that zero is protected: the kernel is even in the sector variable and cannot generate the odd ($L=1$) self-energy a photon mass would require, at any order. Maxwell's equations are the geometry of this phase; the Lorentz force is its geodesic motion.

One consequence deserves its own paragraph because no other framework gets it this cheaply. The inverse-square law is usually tied to space being three-dimensional — so a world with large extra dimensions should show forces falling off faster. Here they don't, and the reason is a theorem: a particle interacting in $d$ dimensions has a $1/r^{d-2}$ kernel, but a three-dimensional observer integrates over the $d-3$ coordinates they cannot see, and the integration restores exactly $1/R$ — for every $d$. The famous $1/R$ is not evidence that reality is three-dimensional; it is what *any* higher-dimensional interaction looks like from a three-dimensional vantage. The extra dimensions were never hidden from force-law tests; they are invisible to them.

## 3. Colour: three classes and an admissibility rule

The $d=4$ geometry has exactly three independent classes — the same three-ness that seeds the up quark's index (Part 2) — and its symmetry group is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$. A quark in that sector carries a three-component colour state because three components is what the geometry provides; the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ of the strong interaction is the symmetry of the space the quark inhabits, not an imposed gauge choice. Down-type quarks, whose three dimensions sit inside the four, inherit colour through the containment.

There is no gluon field in this picture and nothing propagates colour. The quark–quark interaction is the kernel's direct contact coupling, $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-symmetric because the geometry is. Confinement is then not a dynamical struggle but an admissibility rule: the energy of a composite grows linearly with its net colour vector, so the only finite-energy asymptotic states are the colour-neutral combinations — a matched quark–antiquark pair, or one of each colour. A lone quark is not expensive; it is not a state.

The scale of all this is derived, not fitted. The colour energy coefficient $\lambda_c$, built from the confinement scale $\Lambda = N_c f_\pi$ with every factor tracing back to the seeds, reproduces three independent measured quantities at once: the string tension $\sqrt{\sigma}$ (within $0.5\%$ of the Regge value), the Regge slope, and the deconfinement temperature. The *mechanism* question — deriving the linear growth itself from the equation of motion rather than as the unique invariant energy functional — remains open, and is part of the selection program (Part 6).

## 4. The weak interaction: handedness of the complex sectors

Why does the weak interaction see only left-handed particles? In IDWT this is geometry, not decree. The complex (Kähler) sectors — $d=2,4,6,10$ — possess an intrinsic handedness operator built from their complex structure, splitting each sector's spinor into a holomorphic half and an anti-holomorphic half. The weak coupling is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ part of the $d=4$ geometry's internal symmetry, and that symmetry acts on the holomorphic tangent directions only: the left-handed doublet transforms, the right-handed components are geometric singlets — invisible to the weak coupling not because they are forbidden but because the geometry gives the coupling nothing to grab.

The real-sphere sectors — $d=3$ (down quarks) and $d=5$ (neutrinos) — have no complex structure and hence no handedness of their own. Their chiral behaviour is inherited through the complex sectors adjacent to them in the nesting: this is why down-type quarks are vector-like in their own geometry yet couple weakly through the left, and why the neutrino, with no handedness operator available, must be a Dirac particle — the five-dimensional sphere's spinor algebra cannot support the Majorana structure a lepton-number-violating mass would need. The absence of neutrinoless double beta decay, at any order and forever, is this geometry speaking (Parts 1, 5).

## 5. Charges are eigenvalues, not assignments

The Standard Model's oddest inputs are the hypercharges — why $+2/3$ and $-1/3$ for quarks, $-1$ for the electron, in exactly the pattern that cancels all anomalies. Here the pattern is output. The hypercharge generator is one of the eight symmetry generators of the $d=4$ geometry (the diagonal one singling out the fibre), so hypercharges are its eigenvalues; with three colours from the geometry's class count and the lepton coupling $g_{66}=1/n_s$ from the four-class lepton geometry, every Standard Model hypercharge follows, and all four anomaly conditions cancel identically — as they must, since eigenvalues of one geometry's generators cannot be inconsistent with that geometry. Fractional quark charges are not a strange convention; they are what the three-class structure prices a single class at.

The generation count comes from the same accounting. The lepton geometry has four classes; one is the identity class — the constant, vacuum mode, not a particle — leaving three: three generations, because four minus the vacuum is three.

## 6. The coupling numbers

The strengths are as derived as the structures. All couplings descend from the sector self-couplings — fixed by the two seed integers — through one cascade with no adjustable step:

$$g_{44} \to g_s = \sqrt{2g_{44}/\pi^2} \to g_2 = \tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{g_s} \to \sin^2\theta_W \to g_1 \to \alpha.$$

The colour step integrates the kernel over the $d=4$ geometry's volume. The factor $2/3$ is simultaneously the up quark's electric charge and the ratio of the photon's dimensions to the colour count — the same number for a geometric reason, not a pun. The results, against measurement:

|---|---|---|---|
| Weak coupling $g_2$ | 0.65275 | 0.65270 | $+0.008\%$ |
| Fermi constant $G_F$ | $1.1658\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$ | $1.1664\times10^{-5}$ | $-0.05\%$ |
| $\sin^2\theta_W$ | 0.2237 | 0.2229 (on-shell) | $+0.37\%$ |
| $\cos\theta_W$ | $S(76,2)/S(81,2) = 0.88106$ | 0.88108 | exact to displayed digits |
| $\rho$ parameter | 1 (exact) | 1.002 | — |

The Weinberg angle here is a *count*: the ratio of the W's and Z's state counts, squared and subtracted from one. Its $+0.37\%$ residual is a genuine structural residual, stated as such. And $\rho = 1$ is free: W and Z inhabit one sector, so the custodial symmetry the Standard Model has to arrange is automatic.

These couplings do not run. They are fixed geometric numbers at the sector scale; what varies with energy in this framework is geometric dilution — a coupling spread over a mode's $S(n,d)$ states weakens as $g_{dd}/S(n,d)$. Comparisons with running-scheme values (like $\alpha$ at the Z mass) are cross-framework translations and are labeled as such in the first-edition record; done consistently, the fine-structure constant lands where measurement puts it once known vacuum-polarisation bookkeeping is applied on the Standard Model side.

## 7. Quark mixing is a ratio of state counts

When a down quark transitions to a strange quark at a weak vertex, what sets the probability? In IDWT the answer is the same one the Born rule gave in Part 1: relative intensity. A mode spread over $S(n,d)$ states carries amplitude $1/\sqrt{S}$ per state at the shared coordinates, so the transition weight between a lighter and heavier mode of one sector is the ratio of their counts:

$$|V|^2 = \frac{S(n_{\rm lighter},d)}{S(n_{\rm heavier},d)}.$$

Mixing angles are the mass law's own machinery pointed at transitions — counting, the framework's native currency, not a new mechanism. The results:

- **Cabibbo angle:** $\sin^2\theta_C = S(1,3)/S(4,3) = 1/20$, corrected by the curvature of the mediating $d=2$ sphere (a derived $+1/240$ on the amplitude — heat-kernel geometry, no freedom): $\sin\theta_C = 0.22454$ against the measured $0.22450 \pm 0.00044$, a $+0.09\sigma$ agreement.
- **$|V_{cb}|$:** $\sqrt{S(3,4)/S(20,4)} = 0.04116$ against $0.04100\pm0.0014$, $+0.11\sigma$.
- **$|V_{ts}|$** follows by unitarity at $-0.96\sigma$.

Exact unitarity of the mixing matrix is a *prediction* here, not bookkeeping — the framework's first row must sum to one. Current measurements of $|V_{ud}|$ from nuclear beta decay sit about $2.5\sigma$ from that requirement — the known Cabibbo-angle-anomaly territory, where the Standard Model's own independent measurements already fail unitarity at a similar level. The framework agrees with $|V_{us}|$ at $0.1\sigma$ and stakes its claim on unitarity being restored as those measurements settle; if exact first-row unitarity is cleanly excluded, that is a falsification, not a nuisance.

The remaining CKM entry, $|V_{ub}|$, and the CP-violating phases belong to the completion program (Part 6): the quark CP phase has a derived candidate value $\gamma = 67.5°$ with a physical mechanism sketched, and the $|V_{ub}|$ magnitude has a candidate from the same counting law; both are stated there with their open premises rather than claimed settled here.

## 8. The boson family joins

The W, Z, and Higgs are $d=2$ modes, and their indices are joins of the fermion spectrum: each is a fermionic index plus a sector dimension minus one shared vacuum unit — $n_W = 72+5-1 = 76$, $n_Z = 76+6-1 = 81$, $n_H = 15+81-1 = 95$ — with the $-1$ derived (both operands share the one universal ground state, counted once). The same $n_H = 95$ arrives independently as the sum of the three up-type quark indices, and the W–Z index gap of five is the same combinatorial quantity that sits inside the electroweak coupling. Physically, the picture is four-wave mixing: every composition in the spectrum conserves the index budget exactly, with the recoil carried by the photon or by the condensate ground depending on which sector the product lands in. The masses these joins produce were the table of Part 2: W $+0.012\%$, Z $+0.05\%$, Higgs $+0.05\%$.

The mechanism-level derivation of confinement's linear law (the admissibility rule and the scale are in hand; the dynamics is parked with the selection program). The operator-level counting theorem behind the mixing law — the ratio form is verified and its skeleton derived, but its equidistribution premise is a standing parent (Part 6). The $+0.37\%$ Weinberg-angle residual. Charge quantization's closed form — why the topological integers of the fibration yield exactly the observed thirds — is stated as open. Each of these is a gap in derivation depth, not a place where a measurement disagrees: across this Part, everything measured sits within a fraction of a percent of a number that was computed from two integers and the electron mass.
